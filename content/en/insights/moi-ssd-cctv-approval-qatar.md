# How to get MoI SSD approval for CCTV in Qatar

**Short answer:** In Qatar, CCTV in many types of premises must meet the requirements of the Ministry of Interior (MoI) Security Systems Department (SSD). To get approval, use an SSD-licensed contractor, design the system to SSD specifications, submit the design for approval, install it as approved, and pass the SSD inspection before the system is certified.

> **Disclaimer:** This guide is general information, not legal advice. SSD requirements change. Always confirm the current rules with the SSD or a licensed contractor before you design or buy.

## What is the MoI Security Systems Department (SSD)?

The Security Systems Department is part of Qatar's Ministry of Interior. It regulates security surveillance systems, including CCTV, under Law No. 9 of 2011 on security surveillance cameras and devices and its implementing decisions. [CONFIRM: law title and implementing regulations.] It sets technical requirements, licenses the companies allowed to install systems and inspects installations.

## Who needs SSD approval for CCTV?

Requirements apply to many facility types, including [CONFIRM: confirm the current list before publishing]:

- Hotels and hotel apartments
- Shopping malls and large retail stores
- Banks, exchange houses and jewellery shops
- Government buildings
- Schools and universities
- Hospitals and clinics
- Residential compounds and towers
- Petrol stations, warehouses and industrial facilities
- Sports and entertainment venues

If you are unsure whether your premises are covered, ask an SSD-licensed contractor or contact the SSD directly.

## Step 2: Survey the site and agree requirements

The contractor surveys the premises and identifies:
- the facility category and the SSD requirements that apply to it
- areas that must be covered, such as entrances, exits, cash areas, car parks and perimeters
- areas that must not be covered for privacy reasons, such as toilets, changing rooms and prayer rooms
- lighting conditions, mounting positions and cable routes.

## Step 3: Design the system to SSD specifications

The design must meet SSD rules on [CONFIRM: each item against current SSD specifications]:

- **Camera placement and coverage** for each required area
- **Image quality**, including minimum resolution and the ability to identify faces or number plates where required
- **Recording** of all cameras, continuously or as specified
- **Retention**: footage kept for the period SSD sets for your facility type [CONFIRM: period(s); do not publish a number until confirmed]
- **Approved equipment**: cameras, recorders and software from SSD-accepted brands or models [CONFIRM: whether SSD maintains an approved product list]
- **Power backup**: UPS so recording continues during power cuts
- **Security**: access to recordings restricted and logged
- **Monitoring**: a monitoring point or control room where required

The design package usually includes floor plans with camera positions and fields of view, a camera schedule, a network diagram, a storage calculation and equipment datasheets.

## Step 5: Install the system as approved

Install exactly what was approved. If you need to change camera positions or equipment during installation, check whether the change needs re-approval. Keep records of every change.

## Step 6: Test and prepare for inspection

Before inspection, the contractor should:
- check every camera's view, focus and night performance
- confirm that recording and retention work for the full required period
- test UPS backup
- check time synchronisation across all cameras and recorders
- prepare as-built drawings and a test report.

## Step 7: Pass the SSD inspection and receive the certificate

SSD inspectors visit the site and check the installation against the approved design. If it passes, the SSD issues approval or a certificate. ## Step 8: Maintain the system

Approval is not the end. Cameras get dirty, drives fail and settings drift. Keep the system working as approved through regular maintenance. Many facilities use an [annual maintenance contract](/services/amc) with preventive visits and fast fault response. [CONFIRM: whether SSD requires a maintenance contract for certain facilities.]

## Common reasons designs get rejected

- Required areas not covered, or blind spots at entrances
- Resolution too low to identify people or plates
- Storage too small for the required retention period
- Equipment not on the accepted list
- No UPS for recorders and cameras
- Cameras pointed into private areas

## How does PDPPL affect CCTV?

CCTV images of identifiable people are personal data under the Personal Data Privacy Protection Law (Law 13 of 2016). As well as SSD rules, you need a clear purpose, visible signage, restricted access to footage and a retention period you can justify.
