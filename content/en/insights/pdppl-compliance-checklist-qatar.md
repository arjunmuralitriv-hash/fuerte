# PDPPL compliance checklist for Qatar businesses

**Short answer:** Qatar's Personal Data Privacy Protection Law (PDPPL, Law No. 13 of 2016) requires organisations to process personal data lawfully, fairly and transparently, for specific purposes. This compliance checklist covers the essentials: map your data, publish clear notices, get consent where needed, respect individual rights, secure the data, manage processors, handle breaches properly and get prior consent for electronic marketing.

> **Disclaimer:** This checklist is general information, not legal advice. Work with Qatari legal counsel to confirm how the law applies to you.

## What is the PDPPL?

The PDPPL is Qatar's main data protection law. It was issued in 2016 and protects personal data processed electronically, or collected for electronic processing. It applies to organisations in Qatar that collect, use, store or share personal data, including customer, employee, supplier and website visitor data.

Oversight sits with the National Cyber Security Agency (NCSA), which has issued guidelines for organisations. [CONFIRM: current name of the competent department within NCSA and link to its guidelines.]

## Who does the PDPPL apply to?

- **Controllers:** organisations that decide why and how personal data is processed.
- **Processors:** organisations that process data on a controller's behalf, such as IT, cloud, payroll or marketing providers.

Most businesses are controllers for their own customer and staff data, and many are processors for their clients too.

## The checklist

Tick each item, note the owner and the evidence you hold.

### 1. Know your data

- [ ] List every system and process that holds personal data (CRM, HR, finance, CCTV, access control, website forms, email marketing).
- [ ] For each, record the purpose, the types of data, who it is about, where it is stored, who can access it and how long you keep it. This is your record of processing.
- [ ] Identify any personal data of a special nature (see section 5).
- [ ] Identify any data that leaves Qatar, and where it goes.

### 2. Have a lawful basis and a clear purpose

- [ ] Each processing activity has a defined, legitimate purpose.
- [ ] You collect only the data you need for that purpose (data minimisation).
- [ ] You get consent where consent is required, and record it.
- [ ] Consent is freely given, specific and as easy to withdraw as to give.
- [ ] Consent checkboxes are never pre-ticked.

### 3. Be transparent

- [ ] A clear privacy notice tells people who you are, what you collect, why, who you share it with, how long you keep it and how to exercise their rights.
- [ ] The notice is available in Arabic and English.
- [ ] Short notices appear at the point of collection: forms, CCTV signs, apps.
- [ ] Cookie use is explained, and non-essential cookies are off until the user agrees.

### 4. Respect individuals' rights

The PDPPL gives individuals rights over their data, including to [CONFIRM: wording against the law's text]:
- [ ] be informed about processing
- [ ] access their data
- [ ] correct inaccurate data
- [ ] request erasure where the data is no longer needed or was processed unlawfully
- [ ] withdraw consent
- [ ] object to processing in certain cases.

Practical steps:
- [ ] Publish a way to make a request, such as a web form or email address.
- [ ] Verify the requester's identity before acting.
- [ ] Respond within a defined time. [CONFIRM: timeframe set by law or guidelines]
- [ ] Keep a log of requests and responses.

### 5. Take extra care with special categories and children

- [ ] Identify personal data of a special nature, which the law defines to include data on ethnic origin, children, health, physical or mental condition, religious beliefs, marital relations and criminal records. [CONFIRM: full list]
- [ ] Check whether processing it needs prior permission from the competent authority. [CONFIRM]
- [ ] If your website or service targets children, give notice to and obtain consent from a parent or guardian.

### 6. Secure the data

- [ ] Apply suitable technical and organisational measures: access control, encryption, multi-factor authentication, backups, logging and patching.
- [ ] Limit access on a need-to-know basis and review it regularly.
- [ ] Train staff on data protection and security. See [security awareness training](/services/security-awareness-training).
- [ ] Test your controls, for example with [penetration testing](/services/vapt).
- [ ] Build privacy into new systems and projects from the start (privacy by design).
- [ ] Run a privacy impact assessment for high-risk processing such as biometrics, large-scale CCTV or profiling.

### 7. Manage processors and third parties

- [ ] List every provider that processes personal data for you.
- [ ] Choose providers that give adequate security guarantees.
- [ ] Sign a written agreement that sets out the processor's obligations.
- [ ] Check that processors protect data and tell you about breaches promptly.

### 8. Handle data breaches

- [ ] Have a written breach response plan with named roles.
- [ ] Detect breaches quickly, through monitoring such as a [managed SOC](/services/managed-soc).
- [ ] Assess each breach for risk to individuals.
- [ ] Notify the competent authority and affected individuals where the breach may cause serious damage. Regulator guidance points to notification within 72 hours of becoming aware. [CONFIRM: timeframe and threshold with counsel]
- [ ] Record every breach, including those you did not notify, with your reasoning.

### 9. Market responsibly

- [ ] Get prior consent before sending electronic direct marketing (email, SMS, WhatsApp).
- [ ] Identify yourself as the sender in every message.
- [ ] Give a simple way to opt out in every message, and honour it promptly.
- [ ] Keep records of consent and opt-outs.

Qatar's E-Commerce and Transactions Law (Decree-Law No. 16 of 2010) also requires commercial messages to be clearly identified and sent with consent. [CONFIRM with counsel]

### 10. Transfers outside Qatar

- [ ] Know where your data is hosted, including cloud backups and support access.
- [ ] Check that transfers are lawful and that the receiving party protects the data.
- [ ] Prefer in-country or GCC hosting where your sector or clients require it.

### 11. Govern and prove it

- [ ] Name a person accountable for data protection.
- [ ] Approve and publish internal data protection policies.
- [ ] Set and apply retention periods, then delete or anonymise data when they end.
- [ ] Audit compliance at least once a year.
- [ ] Keep evidence: records, consent logs, training records, contracts, assessments and audit reports.

## What are the penalties for breaching the PDPPL?

The law sets fines that can reach QAR 1 million or QAR 5 million depending on the provision breached. [CONFIRM: figures and articles with counsel.] Reputational damage and lost contracts can cost more than the fine.

## Does the PDPPL apply to CCTV and access control?

Yes. Images of identifiable people, fingerprints and face templates are personal data. You need a clear purpose, signage, restricted access, secure storage and a defined retention period. CCTV in many premises must also meet MoI SSD requirements. See [How to get MoI SSD approval for CCTV](/insights/moi-ssd-cctv-approval-qatar).

## Frequently asked questions

### Does PDPPL apply to paper records?
It mainly covers data processed electronically, or collected to be processed electronically. Most modern records fall into this. [CONFIRM: scope wording with counsel.]

### Do we need a data protection officer?
The PDPPL does not use the same DPO requirement as some other laws, but you need clear accountability. Many organisations appoint a privacy lead. [CONFIRM with counsel.]

### Is the PDPPL similar to GDPR?
It shares many principles, such as transparency, purpose limitation and individual rights, but it is a separate law with its own definitions and procedures. Don't assume GDPR compliance means PDPPL compliance.

### How long does it take to become compliant?
A small organisation can close the main gaps in a few months. Larger organisations usually run a 6-to-12-month programme.
