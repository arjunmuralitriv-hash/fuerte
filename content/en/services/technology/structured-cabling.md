# Structured cabling

Structured cabling is the organised system of copper and fibre cables, racks and patch panels that carries your data, voice and building systems. Fuerte designs and installs Cat6, Cat6A and fibre cabling in Doha and across Qatar, then tests every link, including OTDR testing and fusion splicing for fibre, and hands over certified results.

- Site survey, cable route design and containment planning

- Fibre backbones in single-mode (OS2) or multi-mode (OM3, OM4, OM5)
- Fusion splicing, pre-terminated fibre and OTDR testing
- Racks, cabinets, patch panels, cable management and earthing

## Cat6, Cat6A or fibre: which should you choose?

| | Cat6 | Cat6A | Fibre |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bandwidth | 250 MHz | 500 MHz | Very high |
| 10 Gbps distance | Up to 55 m | Up to 100 m | Hundreds of metres to many kilometres |
| PoE suitability | Good | Better for high-power PoE in large bundles | No power |
| Typical use | Desks, phones, basic cameras | Wi-Fi 6/6E/7 access points, PTZ cameras, future-proof floors | Backbones, between buildings, data centers |

For most new commercial buildings, we recommend Cat6A to the work area and fibre for the backbone. Read the full guide: [Structured cabling standards explained](/insights/structured-cabling-standards-cat6-cat6a-fibre).

## What does cabling certification mean?

After installation, every copper link is tested with a cable certifier against TIA-568 or ISO/IEC 11801 limits. Each fibre link is tested for loss and, where needed, with an OTDR. You receive a pass report for every link. Certification proves performance and is usually needed for a manufacturer warranty.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Cat6A worth the extra cost?
For new buildings, usually yes. It supports 10 Gbps to 100 metres and handles high-power PoE better. Cabling stays in the wall for 15 years or more, so it pays to plan ahead.

### What is OTDR testing?
An optical time-domain reflectometer sends light pulses down a fibre and measures reflections. It shows the length, splice losses and the exact location of any fault.

### What is fusion splicing?
A method of joining two fibres by melting them together with an electric arc. It gives a very low-loss, permanent joint.
