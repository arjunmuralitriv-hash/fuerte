## What to do in the first hour

1. **Don't panic, and don't switch everything off.** Powering off can destroy evidence held in memory.
2. **Disconnect affected devices from the network.** Unplug the network cable or turn off Wi-Fi. Leave them powered on.
3. **Don't pay a ransom or contact the attacker.** Talk to us and your legal team first.
4. **Don't wipe or rebuild anything yet.** Evidence is needed to find out how they got in.
5. **Change passwords from a clean device.** Start with email, admin and finance accounts. Turn on multi-factor authentication.
6. **Write down what you've seen.** Times, messages, file names, unusual emails and who noticed what.
7. **Call us.** Then inform your management and legal team.

## How do you know you've been attacked?

- Files renamed or locked, with a ransom note
- Staff locked out of accounts
- Unexpected payment requests or changed bank details in emails
- Your email account sending messages you didn't write
- Antivirus or firewall alerts you can't explain
- Unusual login locations or times
- A supplier or customer telling you they received strange emails from you

## Do we have to report a cyber incident in Qatar?

Possibly. Under the Personal Data Privacy Protection Law (PDPPL), if personal data is affected you may need to notify the National Cyber Security Agency (NCSA) and the affected individuals. Some sectors, such as banking and government, have their own reporting rules. Talk to your legal counsel early. We give you the facts they need.
